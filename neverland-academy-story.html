CHAPTER 1

The Window Wendy Could Not Forget

Wendy Darling returned home because she had to. There were parents waiting, familiar rooms standing quiet, and a life that could not simply be left without a word. She hugged her mother. She listened to her father. She tucked John and Michael into beds that no longer seemed quite large enough to hold all their adventures.

But home did not make Never Never Land disappear.

Peter had told her he meant to stay a boy. At first, Wendy had thought that meant their paths must eventually part. Then, lying awake beneath her own roof, she understood something that startled her: she wanted to stay a child, too.

Not because she wanted to stop caring. She missed caring for the boys. She missed their arguments, their laughter, and the way even the most restless of them grew still when she began a story. She missed Peter most of all.

Days passed. Familiar things felt strange. At the window, Wendy found herself looking past the rooftops instead of down at the street.

Finally, she put on her slippers and walked toward her father’s study. She thought she was going to say goodbye. She had no idea that she was about to ask her family to help build a future.

CHAPTER 2

A Conversation in Father’s Study

Mr. Darling looked up from his papers when Wendy knocked.

“Is something wrong?”

Wendy stood very straight. “I need to tell you what happened to me. All of it. And then I need you to listen to something you may not like.”

She told him about the island, the flying, the Lost Boys, the fairies, and Peter. She told him how happy she had been, and how much she missed them. Her voice trembled only when she reached the part she had practiced most.

“I want to go back.”

“No.”

The answer came before she could finish explaining.

Mr. Darling pushed his chair back. His daughter was speaking of leaving the world he understood for one filled with dangers he could barely imagine. He had already endured the terror of an empty nursery. He would not willingly endure it again.

Wendy tried to tell him that she did not want to stop being his daughter. She wanted a life in which her heart did not feel divided between two homes.

“You are a child,” he said.

“Yes,” Wendy answered softly. “And I want to remain one. But I can still make a promise. I can still learn to keep it.”

He looked at her for a long time. He was not persuaded. Yet he had begun to hear how much this mattered.

CHAPTER 3

The Boy at the Window

A small sound came from the window.

Mr. Darling turned. A boy was standing where no boy ought to have been standing, with the night behind him and a tiny light hovering near his shoulder.

For a moment, Wendy’s father forgot every argument he had prepared.

“Peter!” Wendy cried.

Peter stepped into the study as if arriving through an upstairs window were the most natural thing in the world. Tinker Bell’s light moved over the desk, examined the ink bottle, and darted away before Mr. Darling could decide whether to stare at her or at Peter.

“So,” her father said at last. “You are real.”

Peter looked mildly offended. “Of course I am.”

Wendy could see that her father was startled. She could also see, beneath his stern expression, that he was impressed. Here was the impossible boy his children had described, standing among his perfectly ordinary papers.

But wonder did not erase a father’s fear.

“If my daughter goes with you,” Mr. Darling said, “who will care for her?”

“I will,” Peter answered.

“Who will protect her?”

“I will.”

“This is not a game.”

Peter’s expression changed. He looked at Wendy before answering again. “I know. I promise I will take care of her and protect her.”

Wendy stepped beside him. “And we will not disappear without a way for you to find us. Please, Father. Let us make a plan.”

CHAPTER 4

An Inheritance for an Impossible Journey

The conversation lasted far longer than Wendy had expected. Her mother joined them. Then John appeared in the doorway, with Michael close behind.

“If Wendy is going,” John said, “we want to go, too.”

Michael nodded so firmly that nobody could mistake his meaning.

Mr. Darling sat down again. He looked at his three children, at their mother, and at the boy who had brought a piece of another world into his study. He had been asked to say yes to something extraordinary. If he agreed, he intended to do more than hope it would turn out well.

There would be supplies. There would be arrangements. There would be visits.

The children’s inheritance, he decided, would help fund their return and provide what they needed to live there. Food stores, clothes, tools, bedding, books, and practical necessities mattered even in a place where children could fly.

Wendy had imagined a final goodbye. Instead, her father was discussing how to keep a family connected across an impossible distance.

“Your mother and I will visit,” he told them. “From time to time. And you should not expect every visit to be announced.”

John smiled. Michael reached for his mother’s hand. Wendy felt tears rising for an entirely different reason than the one she had expected.

Her father finally gave his agreement. It was a gift of trust, made with a worried heart.

Wendy hugged him before he could find another condition to add.

CHAPTER 5

A School Built on Neverland Soil

The inheritance did more than make the journey possible. Back on the island, it gave Wendy a way to turn a hopeful idea into something real.

Peter and the Lost Boys could remain children. So could she. But remaining children did not mean they had to remain without lessons, books, or opportunities.

“You needn’t grow up,” Wendy told them. “But the least we can do is learn.”

She did not build alone. Peter and Tiger Lily’s community helped choose a place on land and build the school. Their knowledge, work, and care shaped its timber rooms and paths. Wendy used her family’s support to help supply it: desks, books, materials, tools, and all the ordinary things that make an extraordinary school useful.

It was never a school aboard Captain Hook’s ship. Its foundations belonged to the island.

They called it Never Never Land Academy.

Wendy knew the school would need a teacher who could make room for different kinds of minds. So she brought Teacher Templar to Never Never Land.

Soon, classrooms filled with reading, writing, numbers, science, history, mythology, making, and discovery. John and Michael took their places with Peter and the Lost Boys. Tinker Bell and the other fairies arrived with questions of their own. Male and female fairies alike found workbenches, books, and desks waiting for them.

Tiger Lily and her community were neighbors, builders, learners, and teachers of knowledge they chose to share. Every student brought something worth hearing.

Outside, the island remained wild and wonderful. Inside, it had acquired a new kind of adventure.

CHAPTER 6

The Pirate Who Feared a Report Card

Captain Hook did not welcome the academy.

A child who could read a warning, question a command, work out a problem, or recognize a trick was much harder to frighten. Hook wanted the children’s learning stopped.

But he had overlooked his own crew.

The pirates had always loved Wendy’s stories. They knew the sound of her opening a book. Some had listened from a distance for years, pretending they had happened to be nearby. Now there was a school where they might learn to read those pages for themselves.

Smee came ashore. Others followed.

Hook’s own pirate clan began turning against his plan, not because they had suddenly ceased being pirates, but because they wanted to learn, too.

In Teacher Templar’s classrooms, every earned grade became another answer to Hook. Completed assignments, corrected mistakes, and better results strengthened the school he wanted to close. The students would defeat his scheme with their learning.

And there was still time for enjoyment. Games, stories, gardens, and adventures belonged to Never Never Land as surely as lessons did.

Sometimes Mr. and Mrs. Darling arrived for one of their visits and found Wendy surrounded by books, John explaining something, and Michael eager to show what he had learned. They had not lost their children. Their family had gained another place to belong.

One evening, Wendy stood outside the academy with Peter. Light shone through the windows. Somewhere inside, a pirate was laboriously sounding out a sentence while a fairy waited patiently to help.

“We built a school,” Peter said.

Wendy looked beyond the lit windows toward the paths that had not yet been laid.

“The first one,” she answered.

She was going to build more.
